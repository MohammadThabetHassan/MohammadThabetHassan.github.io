About Me

I graduated in May 2026 with a B.Sc. in Cyber Security from the Canadian University of Dubai (CGPA 3.46 / 4.0). Over the last two years I studied how machine-learning systems hold up against attackers and published what I found: three IEEE conference papers as lead author, with a journal manuscript in preparation.

Hands-on, I work across the full attack-and-defend loop. Offence: web, network and cloud-identity penetration testing with Kali Linux, Burp Suite, Metasploit and Wireshark, including OAuth device-code abuse in Entra ID. Defence: detection engineering, SIEM and log analysis, and digital forensics. In between sits the part I care most about: testing ML models the way an adversary would, and making their decisions explainable to the analyst who has to trust them.

The intrusion-detection and voice-deepfake studies ship as reproducible, CI-tested open-source software with model cards and citation metadata. I also compete: 1st place at the Exploit3rs national CTF (2025) and 1st place at the Dubai AI System Competition (2024), honoured at GITEX. Open to SOC and blue-team, red-team, and AI-security roles, and to research assistant positions, in Dubai or remote.

SmartInject: Automated SQL Injection Testing Using Deep Q-Learning and LSTM-Based Payload Generation Published

M. Thabet, G. B. Satrya, A. Kermani
Proc. 2025 2nd Int. Conf. on Artificial Intelligence, Metaverse, and Cybersecurity (ICAMAC), Dubai, Oct. 2025, pp. 1–6 · IEEE Xplore

A hybrid DQN + LSTM agent, trained in an OpenAI Gym environment over DVWA, reached an 80% vulnerability-discovery rate at DVWA "High", where static fuzzing and a DQN-only baseline found nothing.

OTA Update-Channel Compromise Risk in Smart Mobility: A Testbed Comparing Governance and Cryptographic Controls Published

M. Thabet Hassan, O. Alraas, L. A. AbuSerrieh
Proc. 2026 IEEE Int. Conf. on Smart Mobility (SM), Al Alamein, Egypt, May 2026, pp. 1–2 · IEEE Xplore

A Monte-Carlo fleet testbed (500 runs, 50,000 ECUs). Layered governance cut the median attack blast radius from 27,784 to 46 ECUs and modelled cost from $28.7M to $55K, relative to cryptographic controls alone.

XAI-IDS: A Multi-Dataset Explainable AI Framework for Network Intrusion Detection with a Novel XAI Confidence Score In preparation

M. Thabet Hassan, F. Sadek, A. Sami, M. Khurana
Manuscript in preparation for the Journal of Network and Computer Applications (Elsevier)

An ensemble IDS across CIC-IDS-2017, UNSW-NB15 and CSE-CIC-IDS-2018 (16M+ records, 26 attack classes) with SHAP and LIME explanations. The proposed XAI Confidence Score separates correct from incorrect predictions (Cohen's d 1.0 to 1.2), validated with McNemar's test and paired t-tests.
